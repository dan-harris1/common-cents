Welcome to the V-50 Lectures. What you're about to hear is a revolution. Be prepared to have your mind expanded. After hearing these lectures, you'll never look at politics, or the news, or society, or the world in the same way again.

What's it all about? The V-50 Lectures are an introduction to a new science, the science of freedom. Questions like: How can we stop war? How do we put an end to police states? How can the entire world have prosperity? How can we stop the collapse of civilizations, as Greece collapsed, as Rome collapsed, as America is collapsing right now all around us? These questions have gone unanswered for thousands of years. But here, at the beginning of the 21st century, because of advances in scientific epistemology, they can finally be answered: decisively, irrevocably, scientifically. What does that mean for you and me? It means there's a new way to look at the world, and the future is like none you've ever imagined before.

The V-50 Lectures were created by J. Stuart Snelson in 1964. They were an introduction to the innovative theory of property proposed by a scientist named Andrew J. Galambos. Galambos was a mathematician and astrophysicist. In fact, he was the mathematician and astrophysicist in charge of the ICBM project at the Thompson Ramo Wooldridge Corporation during the height of the Cold War. One day, tired of the idea of armed nations planning to blow each other off the face of the Earth, he pitched a new idea to his boss, George Miller. "Why not send a manned rocket to the moon?" he asked. "We have the technology; here's how you would build it." But in 1956, of course, it was no sale.

Turned down, unable to start his visionary project, Galambos refused to participate in the mushrooming war machine any longer. He decided instead to spend the rest of his life building freedom. He would end police states and wars forever. He would achieve this by using science to explain what freedom is and how to attain it. He set out to change the entire social structure of the world with the power of the scientific method. The result was the V-50 Lectures.

So get ready to see the world from a genuine futurist's point of view. Brace yourself to see beyond the police states and the wars and the politics. And if you want to know about the industries of tomorrow, it's the people who understand the principles in the V-50 Lectures who will build them.

First, a few technical notes about this CD set. The lectures were taped during the fall of 1977 and spring of 1978. The Mapping Freedom Project has done everything possible to restore and enhance the sound quality. In a few spots you may hear minor bits of noise. This is due to the deterioration of the aging reel-to-reel tapes. Aside from these small patches, however, you'll find the sound quality is excellent. Also, the live lectures from which these recordings were made were typically three hours long each. Instead, we've broken them into 30-minute sections and put an ID tag at the end of each section.

And finally, remember: you get out of these lectures what you put into them. Listen to them carefully. Take notes. Give your attention to the arguments. The logic has been carefully crafted. Avoid the temptation to skip around the lectures. After all, you can't appreciate a great movie by watching five minutes of the beginning, five minutes of the middle, and five minutes of the end. If you put in the effort, you will encounter the future.

You will see magnificent cities, environmental rationality, synergistic industries, new technologies, even the economic foundation for useful colonies in space. You will see a world of no intrusive surveillance, no police states, no slavery, no wars. The future is here. Be part of it.
